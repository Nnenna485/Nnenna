# STAGE 1: Angle + Research Spine

**TOPIC:** Why Is Haiti Poor But the Dominican Republic Isn't?
**LENGTH:** 10 minutes

## The two places
**Haiti** (western third of Hispaniola) vs **the Dominican Republic** (eastern two-thirds). One island, one border, populations of almost the same size (about 11–12 million each).

## The moment they were level
**1960:** the IMF found the two countries had roughly the **same real GDP per person** (Jaramillo & Sancak, IMF Working Paper, 2007).
*Honest flag:* some economists argue the Dominican Republic was already well ahead by 1950–60. The script should say "by one IMF estimate", not state parity as fact.

## Today's gap
The Dominican Republic produces **about five times** as much per person (2024: roughly $10,900 vs roughly $2,000, current US$).

## Anchor image
The Haitian proverb **"Dèyè mòn, gen mòn"**: *"Beyond the mountains, more mountains."*
- **Open:** the saying over a sepia relief map of Haiti's ridges, fold after fold.
- **Close:** return to it, with the mountains now standing for the debts, dictators and disasters Haiti kept having to climb, while its neighbour got to climb fewer of them.

## Paradox line
**Haiti didn't fall behind because it started poor. It fell behind because it started as the richest colony on Earth, and everyone wanted their share back.**

## Four threads
1. **Rain:** the trade winds hit the east first. The Dominican side gets more rain, flatter valleys (the Cibao) and nearly twice the land. Haiti is steeper and drier, in a rain shadow.
2. **Sugar:** French Saint-Domingue became a slave-plantation machine. Spanish Santo Domingo was neglected cattle country, and that neglect turned out to be a blessing.
3. **The bill (dated turning point, 1825):** France demanded 150 million francs for recognising Haiti's independence. The loans taken to pay it were still being repaid into the 20th century.
4. **The strongmen:** both sides had brutal dictators. Trujillo (DR, 1930–61) built a state while he looted it; the Duvaliers (Haiti, 1957–86) hollowed theirs out. After 1966 the Dominican Republic got decades of rough continuity: tourism, free-trade zones, elections.
